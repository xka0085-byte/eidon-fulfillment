It Wasn't "Just a Hamster": Keepsakes for Every Kind of Pet

By Eidon, EveryBeloved · October 2026 · 6 min read

"It was just a hamster." "You can get another fish." "At least it was old." If you've lost a small, unusual, or quiet companion, you've heard some version of this — and you know it misses the point entirely. Researchers have a name for what happens when a loss isn't socially recognized: disenfranchised grief. It's one of the most isolating parts of losing a pet that wasn't a dog or a cat.

The attachment was never about size

Veterinary behaviorists will tell you what pet owners already know: the strength of the human–animal bond has little to do with the animal's size, lifespan, or species. A two-year hamster who slept in your palm every night was not a small relationship. A parrot who spoke back to you across the room for twenty years was not a minor presence. Grief scales with the bond, not with the body.

So the keepsake should scale with the bond too. Here's what actually fits each kind of companion:

Small companions — hamsters, mice, rats, guinea pigs, rabbits

  • Tiny paw prints. Their paws are small enough to be reproduced at actual size — a genuinely tiny, genuinely theirs piece. This is the antidote to "just a hamster": proof, in steel, that they were here.
  • A pinch of fur. Small pets leave plenty of fur behind, and a chamber pendant filled with a pinch of it is often more right than ashes.
  • Their photo. Phone photos of small pets are usually close-ups by nature — perfect for a pendant.

Birds — parrots, cockatiels, chickens

  • Their words. This is the one keepsake no dog or cat shop can offer. The thing your bird said — the nonsense phrase, the whistle of your arrival, the word they invented — on a slim bar. It's the sound of them, kept.
  • A feather. Molted feathers (never pulled) can be kept in a chamber pendant, or simply photographed for a pendant.
  • Foot prints. Yes, really — a clear photo of a bird's foot works surprisingly well.

Horses and farm companions

  • Hoof prints. A clean photo of a hoof print makes a beautiful, unmistakably theirs piece.
  • Names, dates, and stable names. Registered horses often have formal names and years — a name bar carries them properly.
  • Ask what it's made of. Barn life means ammonia, moisture and weather — so before ordering, ask the store how the piece is finished, and judge by close-ups of the real thing.

Fish and reptiles

The most quietly grieved pets of all. A betta who watched you for two years, a bearded dragon who recognized your hand — these bonds are real and rarely marked. Their keepsakes:

  • Their photo (phone photos of fish and reptiles are usually excellent close-ups).
  • A simple silhouette or name — a quiet bar that says they mattered without announcing it to anyone who wouldn't understand.

You don't owe anyone an explanation

The mark they left is yours — their print, their face, their sound. Whether the world counted them as "just a pet" is not the measure of what they were to you. A keepsake is a small, private way of saying so. That's really all it's for.

Every companion counts

Paws, wings, hooves, fins — if you can photograph the mark they left, we can make it.
