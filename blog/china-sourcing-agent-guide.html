What Is a China Sourcing Agent — and When You Actually Need One

By Eidon · October 10, 2026 · 8 min read

A China sourcing agent is a local buyer who purchases from Chinese platforms (1688, Taobao, Pinduoduo) on your behalf, inspects the goods, consolidates orders from multiple suppliers, and ships them to you internationally. You need one when you can't pay in RMB, can't read Chinese listings, want someone to QC items before they leave China, or are buying across many suppliers at once. Everything else is detail.

The reason agents exist is simple: the best prices in the world for accessories are on Chinese domestic platforms that weren't built for foreigners. No English, no card payments in most cases, domestic shipping only, and listings full of slang. An agent is the bridge. (If you're choosing between the two platforms themselves, the 1688 vs AliExpress breakdown covers that.)

What a sourcing agent actually does

  1. Finds suppliers. They search 1688/Taobao for your item, often variants you'd never surface with a translator.
  2. Negotiates. Local language and volume relationships get prices and MOQs a foreign buyer can't.
  3. Purchases. They pay in RMB — the part you can't do directly.
  4. Inspects (QC). This is the big one: they open the parcel, check appearance, size, weight and function, and send photos before anything ships. (Why I QC every order myself.)
  5. Consolidates. Five suppliers become one box — one international shipping fee instead of five.
  6. Ships. They hand off to an international line and forward tracking.

What you give up by using an agent

  • A layer of markup or fees. Even a good agent adds cost vs buying direct (if you could).
  • Speed. One more hand in the chain — typically +1–3 days.
  • Less direct control. You're trusting their QC judgment (which is exactly why photos matter).
  • Communication lag. Time zones and language can slow exchanges.

When you do NOT need an agent

  • A single, small AliExpress order with English listing + PayPal — just buy it.
  • Items that have clean overseas listings and buyer protection.
  • Tiny volumes where the agent fee eats the savings.

When you DO need an agent (the three situations)

1. You want to buy from 1688 / Taobao directly. If the cheapest source is a Chinese-only platform, an agent (or a service like Eidon) is the only practical way in.

2. You're buying across multiple suppliers. Five parcels from five factories ship as one consolidated box through an agent. Without one, you pay five international fees and juggle five trackings.

3. QC before shipping matters. For gifts, resale, or anything where a defect is embarrassing or costly, having someone open the box in China and send photos is worth far more than the fee. This is the single biggest reason people use agents — and the reason I built Eidon around it.

Red flags when picking an agent

  • Won't show you the supplier invoice.
  • "Free service" with no shipping rate card.
  • Vague answers to "what's included in your fee?"
  • Pressure to pay outside a tracked method.
