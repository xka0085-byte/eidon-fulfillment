1688 vs AliExpress: Which Is Actually Cheaper for Accessories in 2026?

By Eidon · October 10, 2026 · 9 min read

For the identical accessory, 1688 is almost always 30–60% cheaper than AliExpress — sometimes more. The catch is that 1688 is a Chinese-only platform (Chinese language, RMB pricing, domestic shipping), so the real question isn't "which is cheaper at checkout" but "what's my total landed cost once I get it to my door." Below is a side-by-side that strips the marketing out of both.

AliExpress and 1688 sell a lot of the same items. AliExpress is the English-facing storefront; 1688 (pronounced "yāo-liù-bā") is the domestic wholesale platform the AliExpress sellers themselves buy from. So when you order a "chunky chain necklace" on AliExpress for $14, there's a decent chance a seller in Yiwu bought the identical unit on 1688 for ¥30 ($4) and listed it at a 3x markup. That spread is the entire reason this comparison matters.

The same item, two price tags

Here are real-world ranges I see constantly when sourcing accessories. Exact numbers move with the RMB rate and the season, but the gap is consistent:

None of this means AliExpress is a scam. It means AliExpress is a retail layer sitting on top of the same factories 1688 connects you to directly. You pay for the English UI, PayPal buyer protection, and the convenience of not dealing with China logistics yourself.

Why AliExpress costs more (the fees it buries)

  • The retail markup itself. Most AliExpress accessory sellers are resellers, not factories. They buy on 1688/Taobao and list at 3–5x.
  • "Free shipping" that isn't free. The $14 price includes a thin international mail rate. On 1688 there's no international shipping at all — you arrange it (or your agent does), and it's a visible line item.
  • Currency conversion spread. AliExpress converts RMB→USD at a rate that's quietly worse than mid-market. On 1688 you pay in RMB and convert once, yourself.
  • The "one listing" tax. On AliExpress you can't easily buy 12 different SKUs from 12 suppliers in one cart and ship as one box. On 1688 + an agent, consolidation is normal.

When AliExpress is the better deal

Honesty check: 1688 isn't always the answer. AliExpress wins when:

  • You're buying one item. A $2 agent fee + $4 shipping on a $1 clip can erase the savings. The economy of 1688 shows up at volume.
  • You want PayPal / card buyer protection and English support without thinking about it.
  • You can't pay in RMB and don't have (or don't want) an agent to bridge that.
  • You're testing a product before committing to a bulk 1688 order.

When 1688 wins decisively

  • Anything you buy repeatedly — the per-unit gap compounds fast.
  • Specific or unusual SKUs that AliExpress resellers haven't listed yet. If it's made in China, it's on 1688.
  • Multi-supplier orders you want consolidated into one shipment (see our shipping & consolidation page).
  • When you already have a transparent agent — like ordering through Eidon, where the 1688 invoice price is passed through and the only fee is a flat, stated one.
